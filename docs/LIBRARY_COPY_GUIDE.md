# Dropfall library copy

Updated 10 September 2026.

## Voice

Sound like people who make and play games. Be clear about what the player does, who they can play with and what is available now. Use Australian English. A little humour is welcome when it belongs to the game, but it should not replace an explanation.

- Lead with the game: connect matching colours, race in a slipstream, survive the falling floor.
- Use short, varied sentences. Avoid giving every card the same two-part slogan.
- Buttons describe the action: Play now, Game details, Save, Clear filters.
- Describe a preview honestly. Never imply that a prototype is a finished release.
- Say mobile browser when that is what is available. Do not imply App Store, Google Play or Steam availability without a verified release.
- Avoid unsupported claims about quality, popularity, awards, player numbers or a catalogue being carefully curated.
- Do not call house cross-promotion paid advertising, an optional tip a purchase, or a launch click a completed game session.
- A developer credit is not permission to share tracking, customer records or advertising accounts with that developer.

## Review before publishing new games

1. Play or inspect the current public build, not just the source tree.
2. Confirm controls, supported devices, modes and save behaviour.
3. Separate released features from plans; label previews clearly.
4. Check search, details, accessible labels and no-JavaScript links agree.
5. Check the longest copy on a small phone and desktop.
